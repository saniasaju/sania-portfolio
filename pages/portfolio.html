Overview

I needed a portfolio that could explain how I approach design, connect projects from different parts of my background, and communicate the direction I am growing toward with intention.

The project became a combination of personal branding, interaction design, visual systems, content design, and front-end development. The website itself needed to demonstrate the same qualities I want my work to communicate: clarity, thoughtfulness, restraint, accessibility, and attention to detail.

Challenge

The challenge was deciding what was relevant and presenting it as one cohesive story.

My background includes branding, content strategy, research, front-end development, and UI/UX design. Presenting every part of that background with equal emphasis could easily make the portfolio feel cluttered or disconnected.

I wanted the portfolio to help someone understand what I had done before, what I can do now, and the direction I am working toward. That meant every decision, from hierarchy and writing to motion and code structure, needed to contribute to the same overall identity.

Solution

I approached the portfolio as a digital product built around a clear personal brand rather than as a folder of projects.

I first defined the principles, positioning, and characteristics I wanted the portfolio to communicate. I then translated those ideas into the information architecture, visual system, content, interaction patterns, and finally the implementation itself.

The result is a portfolio designed around one central idea: presenting myself as an intentional designer who considers usability, accessibility, brand identity, and implementation as connected parts of the same experience.

Personal Branding

Before designing the portfolio, I designed the system behind the brand.

The first step was understanding what I wanted my personal brand to represent and how those ideas could eventually become something people could experience. I created a three-component framework to structure the brand:

Internal — The system

The thinking, values, principles, positioning, and decisions that define the brand from within.

Interface — The translation layer

The point where those internal ideas are translated into a recognisable visual and verbal identity.

External — The experience

The tangible outputs and interactions through which other people encounter the brand.

This structure helped me move from abstract ideas about the kind of designer I wanted to become to a portfolio that could consistently express those ideas.

Information Architecture

The homepage works as one continuous introduction.

  • Hero establishes identity.
  • Projects introduces the strongest and most relevant work.
  • Pixels adds breadth by showing visual explorations outside the main case studies.
  • About introduces the person, skills, and thinking behind the work.
  • Contact closes the experience with a clear next action.

Each selected project then opens into its own case study page. This allows the homepage to remain concise while deeper explanations, decisions, and process details live where they are most useful.

Visual System

Editorial, restrained, and structured to let the work speak.

The visual direction was intentionally simple and relied on typography, proportion, spacing, imagery, and movement to align with the brand identity.

Typography

Argesta General Sans

High contrast editorial headings paired with a clean sans serif for body copy and metadata.

Colour

A monochromatic palette keeps the interface calm and gives typography, imagery, and motion more visual weight.

Spacing

4 / 8 / 12 / 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 / 64 / 96 / 120

A consistent spacing scale creates rhythm while keeping the layouts flexible across different section types.

Motion

Motion became part of the hierarchy.

Movement is used deliberately to communicate relationships between sections, guide attention, and create continuity across the experience.

These transitions were repeatedly adjusted so the motion supported direction and pacing. The goal was to identify moments where movement could make the structure of the experience clearer, create continuity between sections, or give an interaction more character.

Development

I vibe coded the final experience.

The website is built with HTML, CSS, and JavaScript using a component-based structure for sections and recurring interface elements such as the navbar, arrows, project section, visual design columns, About section, and Contact section.

AI was part of the development workflow, but the process remained iterative and hands on. I defined how each interaction should behave, reviewed the generated code, adjusted layouts and motion values, resolved interaction conflicts, and continued refining the implementation until it matched the intended experience.

01 — Define the behaviour

Before writing code, I defined what should move, when it should move, and what the interaction was meant to communicate.

02 — Structure the system

Global styles, page sections, reusable components, assets, and case study pages were separated so the project could remain understandable as it grew.

03 — Resolve implementation problems

Much of the development process involved working through stacking, sticky positioning, viewport timing, media fitting, anchor behaviour, component relationships, and interaction states.

04 — Refine the experience

Spacing, easing, reveal timing, focus states, and smaller feedback moments were repeatedly adjusted until the overall system felt cohesive.

Working this way helped me understand how interconnected the entire process is, and how defining the fundamentals early can make the workflow much more efficient.

It also gave me the opportunity to strengthen my existing knowledge of HTML, CSS, and JavaScript through hands-on implementation and problem solving.

Responsive Strategy

Desktop first, responsive by design.

The current implementation focuses on the desktop experience first. This was intentional because several of the portfolio's interaction patterns depend heavily on viewport size, including oversized typography, sticky sections, overlapping transitions, horizontal compositions, and scroll-driven motion.

Rather than simply shrinking the desktop interface to fit smaller screens, the responsive version will reconsider how each experience should behave based on the available space.

The goal is to preserve the portfolio's hierarchy, identity, and storytelling while allowing both the layout and interaction model to adapt appropriately.

Accessibility

Accessibility is treated as part of the interface system.

Accessibility considerations are being integrated into both the structure and interaction behaviour of the portfolio rather than treated as a separate layer added at the end.

Reduced Motion

Motion-heavy sections include reduced-motion states so understanding and navigating the website does not depend on animation.

Keyboard Focus

Interactive elements include visible focus states, while interactions such as the Contact panel account for focus when the interface opens and closes.

Semantic Structure

Sections, headings, navigation landmarks, labels, and ARIA attributes are used to make the page structure understandable beyond its visual presentation.

Clear Interaction Feedback

Actions such as copying the email address provide both visible and screen-reader-readable confirmation.

Responsive Readability

The responsive stage will also consider line length, type scaling, touch target sizing, content order, and how interaction patterns behave on smaller screens.

Reflection

This project reinforced the importance of clearly defining the goal before designing the solution.
  • One of the most valuable lessons from this project was understanding how much easier it becomes to make design and development decisions when the purpose of the product is clearly defined. Once I understood what I wanted the portfolio to communicate, decisions became easier to evaluate.
  • The process of creating this project also made me see the portfolio as a digital product with its own users, goals, interactions, systems, and constraints.
  • Designing and building my portfolio strengthened my interest in the space between design and development. I enjoy the point where code shapes the experience, which helped clarify my direction toward UX Engineering.
  • Rather than seeing implementation as the step that follows design, this project taught me to see it as part of the design process.

Next Steps

I want the portfolio to continue evolving alongside my work. As I gain experience, explore new ideas, and turn more of them into real products, I plan to update both the projects and the portfolio itself based on the context in which I am designing.
